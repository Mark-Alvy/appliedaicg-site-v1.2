## What AACG does, in one line

AACG designs the operating layer around AI for a company, then builds the agents and automations that run on it, so AI becomes a capability the business owns rather than a tool people occasionally use.

## Service lines

### 1. AI Strategy and Roadmap
A practical game plan built through workshops and analysis of the client's own workflows, ending in a custom implementation roadmap with named owners, a baseline, and a measure for each initiative. For leadership teams that want to modernize, cut cost, and build an advantage with a plan rather than a pilot.

### 2. AI Agent Design and Deployment
Autonomous agents built for specific goals, from lead qualification to customer support to recurring reports, with human approval before anything is sent or changed. For sales, marketing, and operations teams buried in manual, time intensive work.

### 3. Intelligent Automation
Systems that fuse process automation with AI to run complex, end to end business processes. For finance, HR, and sales functions stuck in repetitive data entry, document processing, and multi system updates.

### 4. Leadership Enablement Programs
Structured programs that take a leadership team from AI awareness to hands on use of the tools the company already pays for. Three layers, delivered in sequence:

- Executive session. One session, 60 to 90 minutes, for the full leadership team. Resets how leaders think about AI before anyone goes deep on a tool: why productivity gains on old ways of working are the wrong target, why the organizational shift is harder than the technical one, how to pick a domain and redesign the work, and what leaders have to model for adoption to hold. In person with remote leaders joining, or fully virtual.
- Function breakouts. Small group sessions of about five leaders grouped by function (finance, operations, people, legal, and so on), 60 to 120 minutes. Each one is built from scratch for that function: how successful organizations apply these tools in that function, a demo on that function's own workflows and data, and a hands on build where leaders make something they use that week. Governance for that function's data is handled inside the session. A function specific playbook follows.
- One on one sessions. Bookable 30 minute blocks for any leader on their own problem, at their own pace. This is where habits form, because the learning happens on real work the day it is needed.

Between sessions, leaders have direct chat access for quick questions. Every session ends with documentation and a playbook the leader can use the same day. At the end, the client keeps everything plus a usage summary and a recommendation for what comes next.

Typical timeline: about eight weeks, sequenced around executive calendars.

### 5. Executive Events and Workshops
For hosts of executive networking events, leadership offsites, and association meetings who want a working AI session rather than a talk. Three shapes, chosen by the host to fit the room, not a ladder from cheap to expensive:

- The Spark. One 90 to 120 minute session, virtual or in person, with a pre event audience questionnaire and a customized playbook for every attendee: prompt library, tool tiering, executable exercises. Watch and follow format, any audience size.
- The Workshop. The Spark plus a hands on breakout of 60 to 90 minutes where attendees build a personal AI project live and leave with a working artifact, not just a playbook. Three to five hours total. Breakouts work best at 8 to 12 attendees.
- The Program. The Workshop plus a 90 day rhythm: a midpoint group check in, a closing session at the 90 day mark, and a group channel with next day response throughout. Optional individual sessions per attendee.

Every attendee is asked what they want to walk out with, and the host sets the outcome for the room: one personal AI project running by next weekend, a framework for deciding where AI belongs in the business, or an idea good enough to bring to the next leadership meeting.

## What sessions can cover

Drawn in as each function's work calls for it:

- Prompt frameworks: role, context, constraints, output
- Projects and memory: standing workspaces per function and per leader
- Skills libraries: reusable instructions and templates a team shares
- Microsoft 365 connectors: Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, SharePoint, Teams, honoring existing permissions
- Data analysis and dashboards: raw exports to leadership ready views with every figure traceable to its file
- Agentic workflows and automations: delegated multi step work with human in the loop approval
- Small internal tools built by the leader who needs them: intake forms, calculators, trackers
- Decision modeling: options, tradeoffs, and a recommendation from messy inputs, reasoning visible
- Data classification: what goes in, what stays out, by function, inside regulatory constraints such as HIPAA
- Governance and traceability: role based access, audit trails, usage visibility, and how to evaluate vendor AI claims

## How engagements are structured and priced

Pricing is customized to the nature of the service and the client's goals. There is no rate card. The levers that move price are length, in person versus virtual, room size, whether there is a hands on build, and how much follow on time is included. Three engagement models are typical:

- Fixed fee per session or component, with hands on time billed as used. The client sees the running total at any time.
- Monthly retainer covering everything, with open booking windows for one on one time.
- A menu of two or three shapes for an event or workshop, each priced as a range the host refines with Mark.

Travel for in person sessions is at cost.

Every proposal is a first pass refined with the client. Scope, sequence, session count, and pricing adjust as the client's priorities and calendar take shape.

## Common questions and the approved answers

**We already pay for AI tools but nobody uses them.**
Licenses are usually the easy part. Tools get bought, a webinar gets scheduled, and nobody connects the tool to a workflow anyone actually runs. AACG starts from the workflow, not the tool, and every session ends with one workflow redesigned inside the tool the client already pays for, a named owner, and a next step.

**We hired an AI trainer before and it was all theory.**
The test is whether anyone in the room actually ran anything by the end. In AACG sessions they do. Framing, a live demo on the function's own work, then a hands on build. No maturity models, no slides about the future of work.

**Everyone on our team is at a different level.**
That spread is the hardest part to scope, and the program is built for it: one shared foundation in the executive session, function breakouts at the function's own work, and one on one time that flexes per leader. A beginner may need two blocks. A leader who wants agents running on their own data may need several sessions plus restructuring of how their files are organized.

**Our data is sensitive. How do you handle governance?**
Governance is handled inside every session, not as a separate policy exercise. Data classification by function, what goes in and what stays out, human approval before any output moves, and audit visibility. Built to the client's own IT standards.

**Our IT blocks extensions and desktop agents.**
Hands on work does not require local agents. The pattern runs in whatever approved interface the organization allows. Where live execution is restricted, the hands on time shifts to the parts IT never blocks: workflow redesign, the line between AI preparation and human decision, and writing the governance boundary.

**Will you tell us what to do, or just give us options?**
Both. Every roadmap ends in a recommendation with the reasoning visible, a named owner, and a measure. Options are presented so the client can see the tradeoffs, then AACG says which one it would pick and why.
